# Rules, Law & Ethics for Publishing/Monetising AI-Generated Virtual Female Podcast Host Videos (Turkey + Global, as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-12. Most-relevant sources were fetched directly (YouTube Help pages, alomaliye.com reprint of the Resmî Gazete amendment, Houthoff on the EU AI Act). Other items come from search-result snippets of law-firm and news pages and are marked as such where confidence is lower.

## 1. YouTube: synthetic-content disclosure, YPP "inauthentic content", AI personas, podcasts

### Takeaway
On YouTube, a photorealistic AI host must be disclosed ("AI use" = Yes in Studio). Separately, the channel can be demonetised **as a whole** if videos look templated or mass-produced (the "inauthentic content" policy, renamed 15 July 2025). An AI podcast host that presents as a **human expert** on health, legal, financial or political topics makes the channel ineligible for monetisation.

### Cited Findings
- **Disclosure is required** for realistic AI-generated or meaningfully altered content, set at upload via "AI use" -> "Yes" in the Attributes section of YouTube Studio. It must be disclosed when content: makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, alters footage of real events or places, shows realistic scenes that never happened, or uses AI music as the main focus — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **Exempt from disclosure:** clearly unrealistic or animated content; minor aesthetic edits (beauty filters, captions, upscaling, audio repair); AI used for scripts, outlines, titles or thumbnails; cloning **your own** voice for voice-overs or dubs — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **Where the label appears:** on the player for photorealistic content or AI music; in the expanded description for non-photorealistic, animated, or non-music AI audio content. YouTube can auto-apply labels from its own AI tools, from **C2PA metadata**, or from detection. Creators cannot remove labels that come from C2PA, YouTube tools or manual review — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **Penalties:** repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to a forced label, content removal or **suspension from YPP**. Disclosure by itself "does not limit audience reach or monetization eligibility" — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **YPP "inauthentic content" (renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025):** covers content that "looks like it's made with a template" or is repetitive across a channel. Explicitly not allowed: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production", image slideshows or scrolling text with little narrative, and repeated storyline templates. Allowed: a shared intro and outro if the main content differs, and series where each episode has its own concept — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- **AI personas:** "AI personas presenting as human experts on health, legal, financial, or political topics, such as an AI 'doctor' or AI podcast hosts giving financial guidance" are not allowed; "Channels using these are not eligible to monetize." Allowed: "Using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented", and using AI to edit scripts or create backgrounds, as long as the final product shows your creative vision — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- **Reused content** (channel-level): readings of others' material such as website or news text, and compilations without substantive commentary, are not monetisable even with permission — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- YouTube described the July 2025 change as a clarification: mass-produced or repetitious content "has already been ineligible for monetization for years". It said authentic content made with AI tools stays acceptable. The policy works at **channel level** — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)

### Inferences
- A photorealistic virtual female host almost certainly triggers the "realistic" disclosure duty, because viewers could mistake her for a real person. A stylised or cartoon host may be exempt, but disclosing anyway is low-cost and protects against YPP suspension.
- The biggest monetisation risk is templating: the same avatar, the same shot, TTS reading scraped or news text, with only the topic swapped. Each episode needs distinct scripting, commentary or value. Avoid having the host give finance, health or legal advice "as an expert". If those topics are covered, make clear the host is AI and the content is general information.
- Content from tools that embed C2PA metadata will be auto-labelled, so plan for the label rather than trying to avoid it.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch YouTube's podcast-specific rules (RSS ingestion, podcast playlist eligibility) or the status of the "likeness detection" tool. No verified source was found in this pass.
- I could not verify any YouTube policy change after July 2025 specific to TTS or AI voice, beyond the text above.

## 2. TikTok, Instagram/Meta, Spotify

### Takeaway
All three expect AI to be disclosed, and none bans AI or virtual hosts outright. TikTok and Meta require self-labelling of realistic AI content and auto-label via C2PA. Spotify's 2025 rules focus on impersonation, spam and DDEX AI credits for music. I found no verified Spotify rule for podcasts.

### Cited Findings
- **TikTok:** creators must apply the AIGC label when content shows realistic people or scenes. AI content is allowed; the label is a disclosure, not a ban. Content made with TikTok's own AI effects may be auto-labelled. Detection uses C2PA metadata, and TikTok sits on C2PA's Steering Committee (secondary blogs) — [MagicHour](https://magichour.ai/blog/tiktok-ai-generated-content-labels); [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-marketing/is-your-brands-ai-content-ready-for-tiktoks-new-labeling-system/); [Tubefilter, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/14/tiktok-ai-transparency-tools-content-labels/)
- **TikTok ads:** a July 2026 update requires a visible disclosure label on any ad with realistic AI content before it can run (secondary source) — [Stellar Search](https://www.stellarsearch.co.uk/insight/tiktoks-ai-ad-disclosure-rules-are-live-what-brands-running-ai-creative-need-to-do-now)
- **TikTok monetisation:** one seller blog claims that not labelling can cost monetisation privileges. This is **unverified** against TikTok's own documentation — [Rewarx](https://www.rewarx.com/blogs/tiktok-ai-label-toggle-mandatory-stay-live)
- **Meta (FB/IG):** Meta shows an "AI info" label on content it detects as AI-generated using industry signals such as C2PA. It requires users to self-disclose organic posts with "photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio", and "penalties may apply" (post updated 1 June 2026) — [Meta Newsroom, Feb 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/meta-prepares-for-2026-us-midterms/)
- **Meta ads:** auto-detection of third-party AI tool signals, with "AI info" shown under "About this ad" (Feb 2025) — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/)
- Meta renamed "Made with AI" to "AI info" after feedback and Oversight Board guidance — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/meta-tweaks-ai-labeling-after-mislabeling-edited-photos-as-artificial)
- **Spotify (25 Sep 2025):** vocal impersonation in music is allowed only with the impersonated artist's authorisation. A music spam filter targets mass uploads, duplicates, SEO hacks and artificially short tracks. Spotify supports a **DDEX** standard for AI disclosures in credits (vocals, instrumentation, post-production), and these disclosures will not automatically reduce visibility — [Spotify Newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/); [Variety](https://au.variety.com/2025/digital/news/spotify-new-ai-safeguards-28228)

### Inferences
- Treat the label toggle as mandatory on TikTok and IG Reels for a realistic AI host. Disclose in the bio as well ("AI-generated virtual host").
- Spotify's impersonation and spam logic will probably be applied to podcasts by analogy. Avoid cloning a real person's voice and avoid mass-uploading near-identical episodes.

### Gaps
- I found no official TikTok text on Creator Rewards eligibility for AI or virtual hosts, and no official Meta text on monetising AI-generated Reels or virtual influencers.
- I found no Spotify podcast-specific AI policy (Spotify for Creators / video podcasts / Partner Program).

## 3. Turkey: advertising, influencer rules, RTÜK, KVKK, AI bills, tax

### Takeaway
**The biggest Turkish change is in force from 1 August 2026.** The amendment to the Ticari Reklam ve Haksız Ticari Uygulamalar Yönetmeliği (RG 1 Jul 2026, No. 33297) does three things:
- requires ads to disclose "insandan ayırt edilemeyecek dijital karakterler" (digital characters indistinguishable from humans);
- bans ads in which an AI copy of a real person falsely appears to use or recommend a product;
- codifies influencer ad labels ("Reklam"/"Tanıtım", plus a spoken disclosure at the start for audio-only content).

A virtual host doing sponsored segments is therefore clearly covered. Turkish AI or deepfake laws are still **bills only**. RTÜK licensing is a risk for regular, revenue-earning broadcast-like channels. Content-creator income can use the GVK mük. 20/B 15% withholding regime.

### Cited Findings
- **Amending regulation:** "Ticari Reklam ve Haksız Ticari Uygulamalar Yönetmeliğinde Değişiklik Yapılmasına Dair Yönetmelik", Resmî Gazete **1 July 2026, No. 33297**. It amends the 10 Jan 2015 (No. 29232) regulation and takes effect **1 August 2026** (Art. 15) — [Alomaliye reprint](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/07/01/sosyal-medya-ve-indirim-reklamlarina-yeni-kurallar/amp/)
- **Art. 23/A (new):** influencer ads must be "açık, anlaşılır ve ayırt edilebilir". They must carry "Reklam" or "Tanıtım" with the advertiser's name, or "@[reklam veren] tarafından sağlandı". The label must be visually distinct and visible without scrolling. **Audio-only content needs a statement at the start** that it contains an ad or promotion — [Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/07/01/sosyal-medya-ve-indirim-reklamlarina-yeni-kurallar/amp/)
- **Art. 18(8) (added):** ads must disclose AI or software use that significantly affects consumers' economic behaviour, and must disclose when "insandan ayırt edilemeyecek dijital karakterlere yer verilmesi halinde" (digital characters indistinguishable from humans) are used — [Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/07/01/sosyal-medya-ve-indirim-reklamlarina-yeni-kurallar/amp/); [NTV](https://www.ntv.com.tr/ntvpara/influencer-yapay-zeka-ve-indirimlere-1-agustos-freni-1734728)
- **Art. 27(12) (added):** bans ads in which an AI-generated digital copy of a real person "gerçeğe aykırı şekilde" (contrary to the truth) creates the impression that the person used or recommends the product — [Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/07/01/sosyal-medya-ve-indirim-reklamlarina-yeni-kurallar/amp/)
- Other changes in the same package: targeted-ad criteria must be disclosed and changeable, with no profiling-based targeting of children (Art. 25/A); a reference-price rule for discounts; ad bans for medicines, e-cigarettes, tobacco and alcohol (Art. 27) — [Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/07/01/sosyal-medya-ve-indirim-reklamlarina-yeni-kurallar/amp/)
- **The 2021 guide** ("Sosyal Medya Etkileyicileri Tarafından Yapılan Ticari Reklam ve Haksız Ticari Uygulamalar Hakkında Kılavuz", May 2021) bans hidden advertising and requires the ad nature of posts to be clear — [Ticaret Bakanlığı](https://ticaret.gov.tr/haberler/ticaret-bakanligi-sosyal-medya-etkileyicileri-icin-kilavuz-yayimladi); [Webrazzi](https://webrazzi.com/2021/05/06/ticaret-bakanligi-influencerlara-yonelik-yeni-bir-kilavuz-yayinladi/)
- **RTÜK:** under Law 6112, as amended in 2019, RTÜK supervises internet broadcasts with internet radio, TV and on-demand licences. Enforcement so far targets news and commentary YouTube channels, with 72-hour orders to apply for a licence (Cumhuriyet, BirGün TV, Fatih Altaylı in June 2025). RTÜK's president told parliament that channels broadcasting regularly on YouTube should be brought into licence scope (2026 report; date unverified) — [Medyascope](https://medyascope.tv/2025/06/23/fatih-altaylinin-youtube-kanali-icin-72-saat-sure/); [Gazete Oksijen](https://gazeteoksijen.com/turkiye/rtukten-dijital-yayinlara-lisans-hazirligi-youtube-kanallari-da-kapsama-alinabilir-283542); [Habertürk](https://www.haberturk.com/ekonomi/rtukten-youtube-a-ve-sosyal-medyaya-sert-denetim-sinyali-duzenli-yayinlara-lisans-zorunlulugu-geliyor-3900740)
- **KVKK:** the KVKK Authority's "Deepfake Bilgi Notu" defines deepfakes as AI imitations of a person's face, movements or voice, and notes that a few recordings can be enough to make one — [KVKK](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/7804/Deepfake-Bilgi-Notu). Biometric data (face images or prints that uniquely identify a person) is special-category data under the KVKK biometric guide — [Alomaliye on KVKK biometric guide](https://www.alomaliye.com/2021/09/16/biyometrik-verilerin-islenmesinde-dikkat-edilecek-hususlara-iliskin-rehber/). A 2026 Board principle decision (2026/347, cited by a law firm) requires explicit-consent texts to be **separate** from privacy notices — [Gün + Partners PDF](https://gun.av.tr/media/uirdjt3w/fikir-liderligi-kvkk-2026.pdf)
- **Personality rights:** using a person's image in a deepfake violates their personality right in the image (TMK 24). FSEK Art. 86 also protects portraits. Commercial deepfake use can be consented to by contract, but only within the agreed purpose — [DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/2401602)
- **AI/deepfake bills (PENDING, not law):** the first AI bill was submitted to TBMM on **24 June 2024**. MHP MP Halil Öztürk's comprehensive bill would require a "yapay zeka tarafından üretilmiştir" label on deepfakes, removal of rights-violating content within 6 hours, and fines up to 10M TL plus prison terms. A separate MHP bill (İsmail Özdemir) proposes BTK administrative fines of 500k–5M TL for AI videos. A TBMM AI research commission has also been set up. **None confirmed enacted** — [Türkgün](https://www.turkgun.com/gundem/turkiyede-ilk-yapay-zeka-kanunu-tbmmde-uymayana-para-ve-hapis-cezasi/319736); [Hukuki Haber](https://www.hukukihaber.net/yapay-zekayi-duzenlemeyi-amaclayan-kapsamli-kanun-teklifi-mecliste/amp); [Bengütürk](https://www.benguturk.com/gundem/mhpden-yapay-zeka-hamlesi-teklif-ismail-ozdemirden-geldi-sahte-iceriklere-ceza-255430h); [NTV](https://www.ntv.com.tr/turkiye/tbmmde-yapay-zeka-komisyonu-kurulacak,eGOarjtLJEWlU__PcZ-cxQ)
- **Tax:** the legal basis is **GVK mükerrer 20/B**, added by Law 7338 and amended by Law 7491. If income flows into a dedicated bank account opened with an exemption certificate from the tax office and stays under the annual limit, **15% withholding** by the bank is final. The 2026 limit is reported as **5,300,000 TL** by a single advisory source; this is **unverified**, so check gib.gov.tr. Sources conflict on whether 20/B can be used when payments come from Turkish payers who must withhold under GVK m.94 — [Hukukçular Evi](https://hukukcularevi.com/internetten-sosyal-medyadan-kazananlarin-vergisi-icerik-ureticisi-istisnasi/); [Bilal Alyar 2026](https://bilalalyar.av.tr/influencer-vergilendirme-gelir-beyanname-2026/); [Alomaliye, Feb 2026](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/02/21/sosyal-icerik-ureticilerine-yapilan-odemelerden-gelir-vergisi-ve-kdv-uygulamasi/)

### Inferences
- **Do the Turkish ad rules apply to virtual influencers?** Yes, from 1 Aug 2026. Art. 18(8) explicitly covers human-indistinguishable digital characters in ads, and Art. 23/A applies to whoever does the influencer ad. For a podcast host this means:
  - an on-screen "Reklam"/"Tanıtım" label plus the advertiser's name;
  - a spoken "bu içerik reklam içerir" at the start of audio or podcast versions;
  - an explicit "yapay zeka ile oluşturulmuş dijital karakter" disclosure whenever the host is photorealistic.
- The host's face and voice should be fully synthetic, or licensed from a consenting model or voice actor under a written, purpose-limited contract with separate explicit KVKK consent. Never derive them from a real person without consent: that risks a TMK 24/FSEK 86 claim and an Art. 27(12) violation.
- RTÜK risk is low for an entertainment or educational AI podcast, but not zero if the channel becomes regular, revenue-earning and news- or commentary-like.
- 20/B covers income from social media platforms through the dedicated account. Brand deals invoiced by Turkish companies may fall outside it, so take tax advice.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the official Resmî Gazete text or penalty amounts for the 2026 amendment directly. Penalties follow Law 6502 Art. 77, which I did not verify here.
- The 2026 20/B limit is not confirmed on gib.gov.tr.
- I could not confirm the current status of the Turkish AI bills (committee or lapsed).
- I found no AI-specific RTÜK rule.

## 4. Global law: EU AI Act Art. 50, US FTC, digital-replica laws, China

### Takeaway
The EU AI Act Art. 50 deepfake-disclosure duty for deployers has applied since **2 August 2026**. Providers' machine-readable marking has a grace period to **2 December 2026** for systems already on the market. The US FTC treats virtual influencers as endorsers. US digital-replica protection is state-level (TN ELVIS Act; CA AB 2602/1836), and the federal NO FAKES Act is still pending. China has required explicit and implicit AI labels since 1 Sept 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **EU AI Act Art. 50:**
  - Providers must design systems that interact with people (avatars, chatbots, voice assistants) so people know it is AI, and must mark synthetic outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way.
  - **Deployers must disclose deepfakes** and disclose AI-generated text published on matters of public interest.
  - Obligations apply from **2 Aug 2026**. The AI Omnibus took effect 27 Jul 2026 and gave systems on the market before 2 Aug 2026 until **2 Dec 2026** for machine-readable marking.
  - Fines up to **€15M or 3%** of global turnover.
  - Sources: [Houthoff](https://www.houthoff.com/insights/news/ai-act-transparency-obligations-and-the-ai-omnibus-regulation/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)
- Art. 50(1), (3) and (4) were not changed by the Omnibus. The Commission finalised the Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content around 10 June 2026 and later confirmed it as adequate, with final Art. 50 guidelines published in July 2026 (law-firm sources; dates vary slightly) — [Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations); [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/technology/eu-ai-act-code-practice-marking-labelling-ai-generated-content/); [Stephenson Harwood](https://perspectives.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparenc)
- **FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255),** revised and published 26 July 2023: "endorsers" now include virtual influencers that "appear to be an individual, group, or institution". They must make the same disclosures of material connection as humans. Brands must instruct and monitor endorsers — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/07/26/2023-14795/guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising); [InfoLawGroup](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2023/8/11/virtual-influencers-are-now-officially-regulated-endorsersunless-theyre-spokespersons)
- **FTC fake-reviews rule (16 CFR Part 465)**, effective 21 Oct 2024, allows civil penalties for fake testimonials, including AI-generated ones (secondary source) — [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/us-ftc-guides-businesses-on-combatting-deceptive-reviews-and-endorsements/)
- **Tennessee ELVIS Act** (effective **1 July 2024**) adds voice to right-of-publicity protection. It also imposes liability on tools whose "primary purpose or function" is producing unauthorised replicas — [Alston & Bird](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/tennessee-law-designed-to-combat-deepfakes-set-to-take-effect-in-july/)
- **California AB 2602** (digital-replica contract terms must describe intended uses with reasonable specificity) and **AB 1836** (deceased personalities) were signed 17 Sep 2024 and took effect 1 Jan 2025 — [Inside Global Tech](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2024/09/11/california-passes-digital-replica-legislation-as-congress-considers-federal-approach/)
- **NO FAKES Act (PENDING):** reintroduced in 2026 as S.4591 / H.R.8915. It passed the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously in June 2026 and was placed on the Senate calendar on 24 Jun 2026. Daily Journal (7 Oct 2026) reports it "dead for now" after Sen. Cruz objected to unanimous consent. It would preempt some state laws, with a carve-out for pre-2 Jan 2025 laws such as ELVIS — [Sen. Blackburn](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/6/blackburn-coons-bipartisan-bill-to-protect-individuals-and-creators-from-deepfakes-passes-senate-judiciary-committee); [Daily Journal](https://www.dailyjournal.com/article/395055-no-fakes-act-dead-for-now-as-cruz-objects-to-unanimous-consent-request); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/congress-reintroduces-the-no-fakes-act-what-s-new-in-the-2026-bill)
- **China:** the CAC, MIIT and others issued AIGC Labeling Measures on 7 Mar 2025, effective **1 Sep 2025** together with the mandatory standard GB 45438-2025. They require explicit labels (visible text, sound or image) and implicit labels (metadata with provider name or ID). Platforms must check metadata and add visible labels, and users must declare AI content — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1); [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/tmt/2025-posts/China-Releases-Laws-to-Mandate-Labelling-of-AIGC)

### Inferences
- A Turkish creator publishing to EU audiences with a photorealistic AI host is plausibly a "deployer" of a deepfake under Art. 50(4). The safest practice is a visible "AI-generated" disclosure at first exposure, which the Code of Practice supports. Art. 50 also has a lighter regime for evidently artistic, creative or satirical works (disclose in a way that doesn't hamper the work); this was not verified in fetched text.
- For US brand deals, the virtual host needs a #ad-style disclosure. If the audience might think she is real, also disclose that she is AI, because claimed personal experiences ("I use this serum") would be deceptive.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the EUR-Lex text, so the exact wording of the Art. 50(4) artistic exemption is unverified.
- The current floor status of NO FAKES needs congress.gov.
- New York digital-replica and synthetic-performer ad disclosure laws are unverified.

## 5. Ethics, best practice, IP and copyright

### Takeaway
Disclose the AI host everywhere. Design the character so she resembles no real person, and keep the documentation. Avoid sexualised framing. Own the character through human-authored design, scripts and trademark. Purely AI-generated output is not copyrightable in the US; the EU and Turkey require human authorship.

### Cited Findings
- **US:** the Supreme Court denied cert in **Thaler v. Perlmutter** (No. 25-449) on **2 Mar 2026**, leaving the D.C. Circuit's holding (130 F.4th 1039, 2025) that copyright requires a human author. How much human involvement is enough is still open; Allen v. Perlmutter is pending in D. Colo. — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-final-word-supreme-court-refuses-to-hear-case-on-ai-authorship); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/359661/supreme-court-declines-ai-copyright-case); [Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/client-alerts/ip-hot-topic-cert-denied-but-one-thing-is-cert-ain-the-human-authorship-requirement-for-copyright/)
- YouTube itself allows "AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented" but bans AI personas posing as human experts in YMYL topics. This is a platform-codified ethics line — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Turkey's 2026 rules ban AI copies of real people falsely endorsing products (Art. 27(12)), and KVKK flags deepfakes of face or voice as personal-data and personality-rights harms — [Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/07/01/sosyal-medya-ve-indirim-reklamlarina-yeni-kurallar/amp/); [KVKK Deepfake Bilgi Notu](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/7804/Deepfake-Bilgi-Notu)

### Inferences (best-practice checklist; not legal advice)
- **Disclosure:** use a channel bio line, a pinned comment or description line ("Sunucu yapay zeka ile oluşturulmuş sanal bir karakterdir"), the platform AI toggles, and keep C2PA metadata intact. In Turkey, add a spoken disclosure at the start of sponsored episodes.
- **No resemblance to real people:** don't prompt with celebrity names, don't train on a real person's photos or voice without a written licence and separate KVKK explicit consent, and keep design logs and prompt history to show originality.
- **Sexualisation risk:** "female virtual host" content tends to drift toward sexualised thumbnails, which runs into platform sexual-content, ad-suitability and youth-safety rules and can cut ad revenue. The persona must never appear or read as a minor. Keep wardrobe, framing and thumbnails professional.
- **IP ownership:** under the US rule (and probably under EU "author's own intellectual creation" originality and Turkish FSEK Art. 1/B, which requires a work to bear its author's "hususiyet"), raw AI output is weakly protected. Strengthen ownership with human-written scripts, a human-designed character sheet and edits, plus trademark registration of the character name and logo (TÜRKPATENT/EUIPO). Check the AI tool's terms of service for output ownership and commercial-use rights.
- **Trust:** don't fake personal experiences, credentials or "real-life" anecdotes for the AI host. This is deceptive under FTC, EU UCPD-style and Turkish Art. 18(8) logic.
