# "The Skyscraper That Cooked Breakfast" — original script v1 (2026-11-12)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (~60 s, ~3.7 words/s). Our own characters, story and voice. English, no captions.

## Facts (with sources)
- 20 Fenchurch Street, City of London ("the Walkie-Talkie"), architect Rafael Viñoly, still under construction in
  summer 2013. Its south face is concave and focused sunlight onto Eastcheap for ~2 h a day; up to 117 °C was reported.
  (Wikipedia "20 Fenchurch Street"; NPR 2013-09-03; Imperial College news)
- Martin Lindsay parked his Jaguar XJ opposite for about an hour; the wing mirror, panels and Jaguar badge warped/melted.
  The developers (Land Securities and Canary Wharf) paid £946 for repairs. (Global News; Yahoo/AP; New Atlas)
- Ali Akay's Re-Style barbers on Eastcheap: the beam burned a hole in the doormat. (ITV News 2013-09-03; City A.M.)
- City A.M. reporter Jim Waterson fried an egg in a pan set on the pavement in the beam, 3 Sept 2013; City A.M. noted it
  was 347 years after the Great Fire of London (Pudding Lane, ~400 m away). (City A.M. "Eggsclusive"; Wikipedia)
- Press nicknames: "Walkie-Scorchie", "Fryscraper". (Wikipedia)
- Viñoly: "When I first came to London years ago, it wasn't like this... Now you have all these sunny days."
  (Guardian interview, quoted by Gizmodo / archiwik). Also: expected ~36 °C, "turned out to be more like 72". (Dezeen)
- Fix: temporary screen, then a permanent sunshade of horizontal aluminium fins (2014). (Global News; Construction Index)
- Viñoly's Vdara hotel, Las Vegas (2010): curved glass focused sun on the pool deck, singed a guest's hair, melted a
  plastic bag; staff called it the "death ray", management called it "solar convergence". (NBC News; Wikipedia "Vdara")

## Script (≈ 230 words → ~62 s)
A skyscraper in London melted a car parked across the street, using nothing but sunshine.
It's the summer of 2013, and the City's shiny new tower has a giant curved wall of glass facing the street.
Martin parks his Jaguar opposite it and pops out for an hour.
When he gets back, the mirror has drooped, the panels have warped, and the little silver jaguar has melted off its badge.
Next door, the barber's doormat starts smoking.
So a reporter brings a frying pan, puts it on the pavement, and fries an egg, live on TV.
A few streets from where the Great Fire of London started, three hundred and forty-seven years earlier, almost to the day.
The papers rename the building the Walkie Scorchie.
Luckily, London is famous for rain.
Unluckily, the architect said, "Now you have all these sunny days."
You see, that glass wall bends inward, like a giant bowl.
Ever looked into the inside of a spoon? It grabs light from everywhere and squeezes it into one tiny bright spot.
Now make the spoon five hundred feet tall and point it at a parking space.
The owners paid for the car and bolted a huge sun visor of metal fins across the glass.
But the best part? Three years earlier, the same architect's hotel in Las Vegas was frying guests by the pool,
and the hotel didn't call it a death ray. They called it solar convergence.
